1. Where tenders are published

National Treasury's eTenders portal publishes tenders from national and provincial departments and many public entities, with the bid documents to download. Municipalities and state-owned companies also advertise on their own websites. Read the notice for the closing date and time, where and how to submit (some accept e-submissions through eTenders, others want hard copies in a tender box), and whether a briefing session is compulsory.

2. Register on the Central Supplier Database (CSD)

Organs of state use the CSD as the source of supplier information, verified against SARS, CIPC, Home Affairs and others. Many tenders make CSD registration an eligibility requirement and ask for your CSD supplier number. Registration is self-service, in two stages:

  • Stage 1: create a CSD user, activate it from the email link and confirm with the SMS one-time PIN.
  • Stage 2: log in, choose Supplier Details → Add Supplier and complete identification, industry classification, contacts, address, bank, tax, directors/members, commodities and B-BBEE.

You are registered when you receive a supplier number and a 36-digit unique registration reference number. Keep your CSD details current: evaluators compare what you write on the SBD forms with your CSD report.

eTenders e-submissions use the same login: you sign in with your CSD-registered email and CSD password and pick the supplier number you are bidding as.

3. Get a SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN

Bids usually require proof that your tax affairs are in order. On SARS eFiling, open My Compliance Profile, choose Tax Compliance Status Request (good standing) and submit. If you are compliant, SARS issues a PIN that lets the buyer check your current status online with your tax reference number. SARS says there is no charge for a TCS PIN. If your profile is red, fix outstanding returns or debt first; it can take time.

4. B-BBEE and preference points

Under the Preferential Procurement Regulations, 2022 (in force since 16 January 2023), tenders are scored on price plus "specific goals":

  • 80/20 for tenders up to R50 million (all taxes included): 80 points for price, up to 20 for specific goals.
  • 90/10 above R50 million: 90 for price, up to 10 for specific goals.

Each tender states its own specific goals and the proof it accepts. Many use B-BBEE-related evidence. An Exempted Micro Enterprise (total revenue of R10 million or less) can usually prove its status with the dtic's EME sworn affidavit, signed before a Commissioner of Oaths and valid for 12 months; a Qualifying Small Enterprise (R10 million to R50 million) uses the QSE affidavit. If you do not attach the proof the tender asks for, you get zero points for specific goals, but you are not necessarily disqualified.

5. The Standard Bidding Documents (SBD forms)

Most government tenders include these forms. Complete every field, sign them, and date them within the bid advertising period:

  • SBD 1: Invitation to bid, with your details and total bid price.
  • SBD 3.x: Pricing schedule; the total must match SBD 1.
  • SBD 4: Bidder's disclosure, including any interest your directors or members have in other companies (paragraph 2.3) and any links to state employees. It must agree with your CSD report.
  • SBD 6.1: Preference points claim form, with proof attached for every point you claim.

Tenders often add their own returnable forms too, such as a board resolution or authority to sign, a schedule of previous contracts and references, and a joint venture agreement where relevant.

6. Closing dates and submission

  • Note the exact closing date and time. Late bids are generally not accepted, so submit a day early, especially online.
  • On eTenders e-submission, every item in the submission checklist must be uploaded, and only one file is allowed per item (a new upload replaces the old one). Combine multi-page documents into one PDF.
  • Attend any compulsory briefing session and sign the register; missing it usually disqualifies you.
  • Initial any corrections on the forms, and check that totals, VAT and names agree across every document.
  • Ask questions only through the contact named in the tender, before the cut-off date it gives.

A quick pre-submission checklist

  • CSD registration complete, with the supplier number on your cover page
  • TCS PIN (and tax reference number) included
  • SBD 1, pricing schedule, SBD 4 and SBD 6.1 completed, signed and dated
  • B-BBEE proof that matches the specific goals in the tender
  • Every returnable document from the tender's own list
  • Submitted at the right place before the closing time

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a company to tender?

No. The CSD and SBD 6.1 provide for sole proprietors and one-person businesses as well as companies, close corporations and partnerships. Some tenders add requirements such as industry registrations (for example CIDB for construction), so read the eligibility section.

Should I pay someone to register me on the CSD?

Registration is a self-service online process on the official CSD site, and SARS does not charge for a TCS PIN. Be wary of agents who charge to "register you for tenders".

Is a B-BBEE certificate compulsory?

No. Without proof you simply score no points for B-BBEE-based specific goals. Small enterprises can usually use a sworn affidavit instead of a verified certificate.
