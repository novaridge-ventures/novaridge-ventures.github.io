1. The domain name

For a professional identity, get a domain you control (for example a .co.za or .com name) rather than relying only on a free subdomain from a host. Register it in the business's name, turn on registrar lock and two-factor authentication, and keep the contact email current so you do not lose the name at renewal.

Namecheap is a widely used registrar for international names such as .com. For .co.za names you can also use a South African registrar or host, which some people prefer for local support and billing in rand. Compare the renewal price, not only the first-year price, and read who owns the domain after you pay.

2. Where the site lives

A static marketing site or a set of calculators can live on a free or low-cost static host. An app with a database, logins or background jobs needs a virtual server or a managed app platform. The cheapest plan is not always the right one: look at the region (a Johannesburg or Cape Town region, or a nearby European region, usually feels faster from South Africa than a US one), backups, and how you will get help if the machine goes down.

DigitalOcean is a common choice for a small Linux server or an app platform, billed in US dollars. South African hosts such as xneelo, Afrihost or Hex are worth comparing when you want rand billing, a local invoice and a South African support desk. We are not affiliated with those local hosts today; visit their own sites for current plans.

3. Email for the domain

A domain without working email is a liability. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before you send anything important. The free DNSLark checker scores those records and explains what to fix. Hosting the website and hosting the mailbox do not have to be the same company.

4. A simple starting path

  • Register the domain at a registrar you can log into and renew without friction.
  • Point the name at a static host if the site is only pages and files, or at an app platform if you need a server.
  • Add professional email and publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
  • Keep a copy of DNS settings and invoices somewhere that is not only the registrar's dashboard.
